```markdown
# Day 1 (Expanded): Setting the Scene — Context & Tableau

**Objective:** Students will understand the political, economic, and social background of the Opium Wars using embodied learning techniques (mime, tableau) to build historical empathy before deeper analysis.

---

## Detailed Lesson Breakdown

### Part 1: Silent Mime Warm-Up (0–10 min)

**Setup:**
- Ask students to pair up (Partner A and Partner B).
- Explain the rules: no talking, no sound effects — only gestures, facial expressions, and body movement.

**Instructions to give students:**
> "Partner A, you are a merchant. Partner B, you are a customer. Without speaking, act out a trade: A is trying to sell something valuable, and B is trying to negotiate a price. You have 90 seconds."

- Run the mime twice (switch roles).
- Call on 2–3 pairs to briefly demonstrate in front of the class.

**Teacher-led transition discussion (guiding questions):**
- "What made it hard to complete a trade without words?"
- "How did you show trust or suspicion using only movement?"
- "Now imagine two countries doing this same kind of negotiation — but one side has something the other desperately wants, and the other doesn't want to give up its currency. This is very close to the real trade situation between Britain and China in the 1700s–1800s."

---

### Part 2: Mini-Lecture & Discussion — The Canton Trade System (10–20 min)

**Key content to cover (use a visual triangle diagram on the board):**

1. **The British Problem:** Britain loved Chinese tea, silk, and porcelain, but China had little interest in British goods. This created a massive trade imbalance — Britain was paying with silver.
2. **The Canton System:** China restricted foreign trade to the port of Canton (Guangzhou), controlled tightly by the Qing government through licensed Chinese merchants (the "Cohong").
3. **The Solution Britain Found:** Opium, grown in British-controlled India, could be sold illegally in China for silver — reversing the trade imbalance in Britain's favor.
4. **The Consequence:** Widespread opium addiction spread across Chinese society, alarming the Qing government.

**Discussion questions (cold-call or think-pair-share):**
- "Why might a government be worried about a foreign product spreading in its population?"
- "Is it fair to call opium a 'weapon' in this context? Why or why not?"
- "What options might the Chinese government have to stop this trade?"

---

### Part 3: Tableau Activity — "Frozen Pictures" (20–35 min)

**Setup Instructions:**
- Divide the class into groups of 4–5.
- Give each group an index card with **one specific scene** to create as a **frozen, silent tableau** (like a statue/photograph — no movement, no talking).

**Scene Cards (rotate among groups):**
1. *"British ships arriving at the Chinese coast, opium chests being unloaded."*
2. *"A Chinese family discovering a relative has become addicted to opium."*
3. *"Commissioner Lin Zexu overseeing the destruction of opium at Humen (1839)."*
4. *"A Cohong merchant caught between Chinese officials and British traders."*

**Process:**
- Give groups 5 minutes to plan and physically arrange their tableau (using simple props: boxes as "opium chests," scarves, fans).
- Each group presents their frozen scene for 15–20 seconds while the rest of the class guesses:
  - What is happening in this scene?
  - Who are the people, and what might they be feeling?
- After each guess, the teacher gives brief factual context to correct or confirm the class's understanding.

**Teacher tip:** Use a countdown ("3-2-1-freeze!") to build classroom energy and structure.

---

### Part 4: Debrief & Journal Reflection (35–45 min)

**Class debrief discussion (5 min):**
- "Which tableau was the most emotionally powerful? Why?"
- "What perspective was missing from our scenes today?" (Guide students to notice they haven't yet seen the British government's or Queen Victoria's perspective — this sets up Day 2.)

**Journal Writing Prompt (10 min, individual, collected for formative assessment):**
> "Imagine you are a Qing government official in 1839, tasked with stopping the opium trade. Write 3–4 sentences describing what you see in your city, how you feel about it, and what action you would recommend to the Emperor."

**Assessment focus:** Look for historical grounding (reference to addiction, foreign trade, government authority) and emotional/perspective depth.

---

## Homework (Assigned at the End of Day 1)

**Reading:** Excerpt from Commissioner Lin Zexu's 1839 letter to Queen Victoria (provided handout).

**Homework Questions** (to be answered in complete sentences, due at the start of Day 2):

1. According to Lin Zexu's letter, what specific arguments does he make to try to convince Queen Victoria to stop the opium trade?

2. How does Lin Zexu describe the moral responsibility of the British government in this letter? Do you think his argument is persuasive? Why or why not?

3. If you were Queen Victoria receiving this letter, what would be your biggest concern in deciding how to respond — moral, economic, or political? Explain your reasoning in 2–3 sentences.
```
