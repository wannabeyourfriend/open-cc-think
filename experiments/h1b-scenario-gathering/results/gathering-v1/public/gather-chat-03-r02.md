# Day 1 (Expanded): Setting the Scene — Causes of the Opium Wars

**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Learning Focus:** Trade imbalance, the Canton System, and the rise of the opium trade as causes of conflict.

---

## Pre-Class Setup
- Arrange desks/chairs to create open floor space for tableau work.
- Post the trade triangle diagram (Britain → India: opium; India → China: opium; China → Britain: tea, silk, silver) on the board, but keep it covered until the mini-lecture.
- Prepare 5–6 role/topic cards for tableau groups (see below).
- Have soft background music ready (optional) to signal transitions into and out of freeze poses.

---

## 1. Hook: Silent Story (0–5 min)
**Teacher-led mime, no talking.**

Script/Action sequence for teacher:
1. Mime pouring and sipping tea contentedly (5 sec).
2. Mime receiving a bag of silver coins, smiling, weighing it in hands (5 sec).
3. Mime handing over a small wrapped "package" (opium) secretively, looking over shoulder (5 sec).
4. Mime smoking a pipe, gradually slowing movements, swaying, and collapsing into a chair (10 sec).
5. Freeze in the collapsed position for 5 seconds.

**Discussion prompt after the mime:**
- "What story did my body just tell, without any words?"
- "What do you think each object represented — the tea, the silver, the package, the pipe?"
- Take 2–3 quick student guesses before revealing the topic: *the opium trade between Britain and China*.

---

## 2. Mini-Lecture: Trade Imbalance & the Canton System (5–17 min)
**Content to cover (with guiding questions to keep it interactive rather than pure lecture):**

- **The British "tea problem":** Britain imported massive quantities of Chinese tea, silk, and porcelain but had few goods China wanted in return. Ask: *"If you love something but have nothing the seller wants, what do you do?"*
- **The Canton System:** China restricted all foreign trade to the single port of Canton (Guangzhou), and foreign merchants operated under strict Qing supervision through licensed Chinese merchants (the *Cohong*). Draw a simple map/diagram showing Canton as the only trade gateway.
- **Silver drain:** Britain paid for tea largely in silver, causing a growing trade deficit. Show/board a simple number: by the late 1700s, Britain was paying millions of silver taels annually.
- **The opium "solution":** The British East India Company began growing opium in India and smuggling it into China to reverse the silver flow. Show the import numbers:
  - ~1,000 chests/year in the 1780s
  - ~40,000 chests/year by 1838
- **China's response:** Opium addiction was spreading rapidly and alarming the Qing court. Emperor Daoguang appointed Commissioner **Lin Zexu** in 1839 to eliminate the opium trade.

**Reveal the trade triangle diagram** on the board and have students copy it into notes.

---

## 3. Tableau Activity (17–33 min)

### Step-by-step instructions:
1. **Grouping (2 min):** Divide class into 5–6 groups of 4–5 students.
2. **Assign scenes (1 min):** Hand each group a card with one of these three prompts (some groups may repeat a prompt if class size requires):
   - **Scene A:** "A bustling Chinese port before the opium trade — merchants, tea traders, daily life."
   - **Scene B:** "The spread of opium addiction in Chinese society — families, addicts, hidden dens."
   - **Scene C:** "British and Company merchants profiting from opium sales — counting silver, loading ships."
3. **Planning time (5 min):** Groups discuss and physically arrange themselves into a **frozen tableau** (a silent, still "living picture") representing their scene. Remind them: use levels (standing, kneeling, crouching), facial expression, and spacing to show relationships and power.
4. **Presentations (8 min):** Each group presents their frozen tableau for 10–15 seconds while the rest of the class observes silently.
5. **Thought-Tap technique (remaining time):** After each tableau is shown, the teacher (or classmates) gently taps a student's shoulder; that student says **one line** revealing their character's inner thought (e.g., a tapped "addict" might say, *"I can't stop... I need more"*; a tapped "merchant" might say, *"This silver will make me rich"*).

**Teacher circulates during planning** to ask guiding questions: *"Who has the most power in this image? How can you show that with your body?"*

---

## 4. Debrief Discussion (33–41 min)

Guiding questions to write on the board and discuss as a class:
- What emotions or power dynamics did you notice in the tableaux?
- Which characters seemed powerful? Which seemed powerless? Why?
- How does seeing this "frozen" helps you understand the human impact of a trade policy, compared to just reading statistics?
- Connect back to the numbers: *"We said opium imports rose from 1,000 to 40,000 chests a year. How does that number feel different now that you've seen it acted out?"**
- Introduce the term **"unequal exchange"** and ask students to define it in their own words based on what they just performed.

---

## 5. Exit Ticket (41–45 min)

On an index card or slip of paper, students respond to:
1. Name **two causes** of the growing conflict between Britain and China.
2. Write **one question** you still have about the opium trade or the coming war.

Collect exit tickets as a quick formative assessment before Day 2.

---

## Homework: Reading + Questions

**Assigned Reading:** Excerpt from Commissioner **Lin Zexu's 1839 letter to Queen Victoria** (provide translated excerpt handout).

**Homework Questions** (to be answered in complete sentences, submitted at start of Day 2):

1. According to Lin Zexu's letter, what moral or ethical argument does he make against the British opium trade? Quote at least one phrase from the letter to support your answer.
2. How does Lin Zexu describe the effects of opium on Chinese society and individuals? Why might he feel these effects justify strong action against British merchants?
3. Based on the tone and content of the letter, predict how the British government might respond to Lin Zexu's demands. What reasons might they give for continuing or defending the opium trade?
