```markdown
# Day 1 (Expanded): Setting the Stage — Trade, Tension, and Tableau

**Focus:** Causes of the Opium War — the tea/silver/opium trade triangle, economic imbalance, and cultural misunderstanding between Britain and China.

**Learning Objectives for Today:**
- Identify the key goods and players involved in the 18th–19th century trade between Britain, India, and China.
- Explain why Britain turned to opium as a "solution" to its trade deficit.
- Use nonverbal communication (mime) and tableau to represent historical relationships and tensions.

**Materials for Day 1:**
- Paper cutouts labeled "Silver," "Tea," "Silk," "Opium" (enough for each pair/group)
- Role cards: "British Trader," "Chinese Merchant," "Qing Official," "British East India Company Agent," "Indian Opium Farmer"
- Chart paper and markers (one set per group)
- Timer/stopwatch
- Whiteboard for vocabulary

---

## Detailed Lesson Sequence

### 1. Warm-Up: "Frozen Trade" (10 minutes)

**Setup (2 min):**
- Pair students up. Give each pair a small set of paper props (tea leaves, silver coins, silk, opium packet cutouts).
- Assign roles: Partner A = British Trader, Partner B = Chinese Merchant.

**Activity (5 min):**
- **Rule: No talking.** Using only gestures, facial expressions, and props, each pair must "negotiate" a trade exchange.
- Teacher calls out prompts one at a time (30 seconds each) to guide the improvisation:
  - "Trade tea for silver."
  - "Now the British trader has no more silver — what do they offer instead?"
  - "The Chinese merchant refuses the new offer. Show your reaction."

**Debrief (3 min) — Whole Class Discussion:**
- "What was difficult about communicating without words?"
- "How did it feel to run out of something valuable to trade?"
- "Real historical trade involved not just language barriers, but very different worldviews. Keep that in mind today."

*Teacher Note:* This warm-up plants the seed for the trade imbalance concept before it's formally introduced.

---

### 2. Direct Instruction: The Trade Imbalance (10 minutes)

**Mini-Lecture Outline (use whiteboard notes as you go):**
- Britain loves Chinese tea, silk, and porcelain → demand is high in Britain.
- China wants very little from Britain in return → China only accepts **silver** as payment.
- Result: Britain's silver reserves are draining fast — a **trade deficit**.
- Britain's solution: grow **opium** in British-controlled India, smuggle it into China, sell it for silver, use that silver to buy tea.
- Consequence: widespread opium addiction spreads in China; the Qing government becomes alarmed.

**Vocabulary to introduce (write on board, students copy into notes):**
- *Trade deficit* — importing more than exporting, resulting in an outflow of money/silver.
- *Contraband* — illegal goods smuggled into a country.
- *Sovereignty* — a nation's right to govern itself without outside interference.

**Quick Check for Understanding (2 min):**
- Cold-call 2–3 students: "In your own words, why did Britain start smuggling opium into China?"

---

### 3. Main Activity: "Living Tableau — The Opium Triangle" (20 minutes)

**Step 1 — Group Formation (3 min):**
- Divide class into 3 groups of roughly equal size:
  - **Group 1: Britain** (merchants, government officials, East India Company)
  - **Group 2: India** (opium farmers, laborers forced to grow poppies)
  - **Group 3: China** (Qing officials, addicted citizens, worried families)

**Step 2 — Tableau Preparation (7 min):**
- Each group receives a short role-description card explaining their group's position in the triangle trade.
- Groups must create a **frozen tableau** (a silent, still "living picture") using their bodies to show:
  - Who they are
  - Their relationship to the opium trade
  - One emotion they feel about their situation (pride, guilt, desperation, anger, etc.)
- Encourage use of levels (standing, kneeling, reaching) and use of the paper props from the warm-up.

**Step 3 — Performance & "Thought-Tap" (8 min):**
- Groups present their tableau one at a time (30 seconds of silence/stillness).
- Teacher walks around and gently taps a student on the shoulder; that student must say **one line in character** revealing their thoughts or feelings (e.g., "I never wanted to grow this poppy, but my family needs to eat" or "This silver keeps our empire funded").
- After all three tableaux have been shown individually, **combine all three groups into one large connected tableau** to visually represent the full triangle trade (Britain ↔ India ↔ China).

**Step 4 — Whole Class Discussion (2 min):**
- "Looking at the combined tableau, who seems to have the most power? The least?"
- "Does anyone in this triangle seem trapped or without real choices?"

*Teacher Note:* This activity builds empathy across multiple perspectives simultaneously and previews the complexity of assigning "blame" — useful for Day 2's trial activity.

---

### 4. Closing / Exit Ticket (5 minutes)

- Students individually write **one sentence** on an index card or scrap paper:
  *"Based on today's lesson, why do you think tensions were rising between Britain and China by the 1830s?"*
- Collect as a quick formative assessment to check understanding before Day 2.

---

## Differentiation Notes for Day 1
- **Struggling learners:** Provide a printed sentence starter for the "thought-tap" line (e.g., "I feel ___ because ___.")
- **Advanced learners:** Challenge them to include a specific historical detail in their tableau line (a real statistic, event, or quote).
- **English Language Learners:** Pair with a peer for the mime warm-up; allow tableau lines to be prepared in writing beforehand.

---

## Homework Questions (Day 1)

1. In your own words, explain what a "trade deficit" is and why Britain experienced one with China in the 1700s–1800s.
2. Why do you think the Chinese government eventually became alarmed about the opium trade? Consider both economic and social effects.
3. Imagine you are a British merchant profiting from the opium trade. Write a short paragraph (5–6 sentences) justifying your actions from your own perspective — even if you know today it caused great harm.
```
